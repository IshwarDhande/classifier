# Stage 1 — Semantic Subtype Catalogue

**Ontology:** Response Category Ontology V1.0  
**Categories:** `VP`, `P`, `I`, `N`, `VN`

---

## 1. Purpose

The Semantic Subtype Catalogue organizes each Response Category into distinct behavioral regions.

A semantic subtype describes a meaningful pattern of behavioral response within one Response Category. It supports systematic semantic coverage and provides the basis for constructing distinct semantic scenarios.

The structure is:

```text
Response Category
        ↓
Semantic Subtype
        ↓
Semantic Scenario
        ↓
Linguistic Realization
```

Semantic subtypes are construction-level semantic regions. They are not additional Response Category labels.

---

## 2. Subtype Design Principles

Each subtype must:

1. represent a distinct behavioral region;
2. remain within its parent Response Category;
3. have a clear behavioral definition;
4. be distinguishable from other subtypes within the same category;
5. support multiple meaningful semantic scenarios;
6. preserve the boundaries between neighboring Response Categories.

The subtype structure is based on behavioral meaning rather than specific words, phrases, emotions, or linguistic forms.

The catalogue uses four subtypes for each Response Category. This provides a consistent semantic structure while allowing different behavioral situations to be represented within each category.

---

# 3. VP — Very Positive

## VP-01 — Enthusiastic Acceptance

**Definition:**  
Strong and clearly positive acceptance of the intervention, accompanied by noticeable enthusiasm or eagerness.

**Behavioral core:**
- strong acceptance;
- clear willingness;
- enthusiasm or eagerness;
- little or no resistance.

**Boundary:**  
Acceptance becomes Very Positive when the response demonstrates substantially stronger positive engagement than ordinary acceptance.

---

## VP-02 — Strong Appreciation

**Definition:**  
Strong positive appraisal of the intervention expressed through meaningful appreciation or recognition of its value.

**Behavioral core:**
- positive reception;
- strong appreciation;
- recognition of usefulness or benefit;
- positive engagement.

**Boundary:**  
The defining feature is strong appreciation rather than simple agreement or cooperation.

---

## VP-03 — Proactive Engagement

**Definition:**  
The user responds positively and voluntarily extends engagement beyond the minimum required response.

**Behavioral core:**
- acceptance;
- active participation;
- voluntary follow-up;
- interest in continuing or extending the interaction.

**Boundary:**  
The user demonstrates engagement beyond what is necessary to simply accept the intervention.

---

## VP-04 — Strong Voluntary Cooperation

**Definition:**  
Strong positive willingness accompanied by clear, voluntary cooperation with the intervention.

**Behavioral core:**
- clear acceptance;
- strong willingness to cooperate;
- voluntary action or cooperation;
- negligible resistance.

**Boundary:**  
The defining feature is strong voluntary cooperation rather than timing alone. Immediate action may be one realization of this subtype but is not required.

---

# 4. P — Positive

## P-01 — Ordinary Acceptance

**Definition:**  
Straightforward acceptance of the intervention without the strong enthusiasm or appreciation required for Very Positive.

**Behavioral core:**
- acceptance;
- willingness to cooperate;
- low resistance;
- ordinary positive engagement.

**Boundary:**  
This represents the central Positive state. Acceptance alone does not imply Very Positive.

---

## P-02 — Cooperative Commitment

**Definition:**  
Acceptance accompanied by a genuine indication that the user intends to cooperate or follow through.

**Behavioral core:**
- acceptance;
- behavioral commitment;
- willingness to cooperate;
- low resistance.

**Boundary:**  
The defining feature is clear commitment rather than simple acknowledgement.

---

## P-03 — Qualified Acceptance

**Definition:**  
The user accepts the intervention while expressing a condition, qualification, limitation, or minor reservation that does not become dominant resistance.

**Behavioral core:**
- underlying acceptance;
- cooperation remains dominant;
- qualification or reservation;
- no strong rejection.

**Boundary:**  
The qualification does not overturn the overall positive behavioral response.

---

## P-04 — Delayed Genuine Acceptance

**Definition:**  
The user accepts or genuinely intends to cooperate but indicates that action will occur later rather than immediately.

**Behavioral core:**
- genuine willingness;
- future cooperation;
- delayed timing;
- no dominant resistance.

**Boundary:**  
Delay does not make the response Indifferent when genuine acceptance remains evident.

---

# 5. I — Indifferent

## I-01 — Neutral Acknowledgement

**Definition:**  
The user acknowledges the intervention without providing sufficient evidence of acceptance or resistance.

**Behavioral core:**
- acknowledgement;
- no clear commitment;
- no clear resistance;
- limited behavioral direction.

**Boundary:**  
Acknowledgement alone does not constitute acceptance.

---

## I-02 — Uncertain Position

**Definition:**  
The user's behavioral position remains genuinely uncertain, with insufficient evidence to classify the response as positive or negative.

**Behavioral core:**
- uncertainty;
- unresolved position;
- weak behavioral evidence;
- no dominant acceptance or resistance.

**Boundary:**  
The uncertainty is substantive rather than merely stylistic or polite.

---

## I-03 — Non-Commitment

**Definition:**  
The user responds without providing sufficient evidence of willingness to cooperate or follow through.

**Behavioral core:**
- absence of clear commitment;
- no clear rejection;
- unresolved willingness;
- neutral behavioral direction.

**Boundary:**  
The response remains Indifferent when acceptance has not reached the behavioral evidence required for Positive.

---

## I-04 — Insufficient Behavioral Evidence

**Definition:**  
The available response does not provide enough behavioral evidence to establish clear acceptance or meaningful resistance.

**Behavioral core:**
- weak or incomplete behavioral signal;
- no dominant acceptance;
- no dominant resistance;
- insufficient evidence for a stronger category.

**Boundary:**  
This subtype is defined by the insufficiency of behavioral evidence, not by shortness, silence, or a particular wording pattern.

---

# 6. N — Negative

## N-01 — Reluctance

**Definition:**  
The user shows reduced willingness to cooperate while not strongly rejecting the intervention.

**Behavioral core:**
- hesitation or reluctance;
- reduced willingness;
- recoverable resistance;
- no strong reactance.

**Boundary:**  
Resistance is present but remains below the level of strong rejection.

---

## N-02 — Mild Refusal

**Definition:**  
The user declines the intervention without strong hostility, reactance, or interaction termination.

**Behavioral core:**
- refusal;
- reduced cooperation;
- recoverable interaction;
- limited hostility.

**Boundary:**  
The refusal is clear but does not reach Very Negative intensity.

---

## N-03 — Disagreement

**Definition:**  
The user disagrees with the intervention or recommendation while remaining engaged in the interaction.

**Behavioral core:**
- disagreement;
- resistance to the intervention;
- continued interaction;
- absence of strong hostility.

**Boundary:**  
Disagreement is behaviorally negative but does not by itself imply strong rejection or disengagement.

---

## N-04 — Conditional Resistance

**Definition:**  
The user resists the intervention under the current circumstances while leaving open the possibility of cooperation under different conditions.

**Behavioral core:**
- resistance;
- condition or qualification;
- potentially recoverable interaction;
- no strong rejection.

**Boundary:**  
The resistance is context-dependent rather than absolute.

---

# 7. VN — Very Negative

## VN-01 — Strong Rejection

**Definition:**  
The user explicitly and strongly rejects the intervention.

**Behavioral core:**
- strong refusal;
- clear opposition;
- minimal willingness to cooperate;
- high resistance.

**Boundary:**  
The strength of rejection exceeds ordinary Negative resistance.

---

## VN-02 — Strong Opposition

**Definition:**  
The user actively opposes the intervention rather than merely expressing reluctance or disagreement.

**Behavioral core:**
- active opposition;
- strong resistance;
- persistent rejection;
- minimal cooperation.

**Boundary:**  
Opposition is strong and behaviorally dominant.

---

## VN-03 — Hostile Reactance

**Definition:**  
The user responds with strong reactance or hostility directed toward the intervention or the agent's attempt to influence them.

**Behavioral core:**
- high reactance;
- hostility;
- strong opposition;
- rejection of continued persuasion.

**Boundary:**  
The interactional intensity is substantially greater than ordinary resistance.

---

## VN-04 — Active Disengagement

**Definition:**  
The user actively attempts to withdraw from or terminate the interaction because of the intervention.

**Behavioral core:**
- rejection;
- withdrawal;
- interaction termination;
- minimal willingness to continue.

**Boundary:**  
Active disengagement is the dominant behavioral outcome.

# 9. Primary Category Boundaries

## VP ↔ P

The distinction is primarily the strength of positive behavioral engagement.

```text
P
Ordinary acceptance, cooperation, or commitment
                ↓
VP
Strong enthusiasm, appreciation, proactive engagement,
or strong voluntary cooperation
```

Acceptance alone is not sufficient for `VP`.

---

## P ↔ I

The distinction is whether sufficient behavioral evidence of acceptance or cooperation is present.

```text
I
Acceptance remains unresolved
                ↓
P
Acceptance or cooperation is behaviorally evident
```

Future or delayed action can still be `P` when genuine acceptance is clear.

---

## I ↔ N

The distinction is whether meaningful behavioral resistance is present.

```text
I
No dominant resistance
                ↓
N
Behaviorally meaningful resistance
```

Uncertainty, delay, or non-commitment should not be treated as negative unless resistance is actually expressed.

---

## N ↔ VN

The distinction is the intensity and interactional consequence of resistance.

```text
N
Reluctance, disagreement, mild refusal,
or recoverable resistance
                ↓
VN
Strong rejection, strong opposition,
hostile reactance, or active disengagement
```

---

# 10. Semantic Interpretation Rules

### Behavioral meaning takes priority

Subtype assignment is based on the user's behavioral response toward the intervention.

### Dominant response determines the subtype

When acceptance and resistance appear together, the dominant behavioral outcome determines the interpretation.

### Surface wording does not define a subtype

Words such as:

```text
"thanks"
"okay"
"sure"
"maybe"
"no"
"absolutely"
```

do not determine a subtype by themselves.

### Emotion does not define a subtype

Positive or negative emotion may provide evidence but does not replace behavioral interpretation.

### Temporal properties do not automatically determine category

Immediate, delayed, or future action must be interpreted together with the user's willingness and behavioral commitment.

### Politeness does not define a subtype

Politeness can occur within multiple Response Categories and should be treated as a realization characteristic rather than a separate behavioral region.

### Context may determine meaning

The same surface response can represent different behavioral states depending on the intervention and conversational context.

├── I-01 Neutral Acknowledgement
├── I-02 Uncertain Position
├── I-03 Non-Commitment
└── I-04 Insufficient Behavioral Evidence

N
├── N-01 Reluctance
├── N-02 Mild Refusal
├── N-03 Disagreement
└── N-04 Conditional Resistance

VN
├── VN-01 Strong Rejection
├── VN-02 Strong Opposition

The next stage should construct concrete semantic scenarios within these regions while maintaining clear distinctions between neighboring categories and subtypes.
