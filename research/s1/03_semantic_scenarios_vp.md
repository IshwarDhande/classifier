# Stage 1 — Semantic Scenario Inventory: VP

**Response Category:** `VP` — Very Positive  
**Semantic Subtypes:** 4  
**Scenario Count:** 20

## 1. Purpose

This inventory defines distinct behavioral situations in which a user's response belongs to the `VP` Response Category.

`VP` represents a strongly positive behavioral reaction to the intervention. The response demonstrates strong acceptance, appreciation, enthusiasm, proactive engagement, or strong voluntary cooperation.

The scenarios below describe semantic situations, not linguistic realizations.

---

# 2. VP-01 — Enthusiastic Acceptance

## VP-01-S01 — Eager Acceptance of Recommendation

**Scenario:** The user readily accepts the intervention and expresses clear eagerness to follow the recommendation.

**Behavioral core:** Strong acceptance + eagerness.

**Boundary:** Stronger positive engagement than ordinary acceptance.

## VP-01-S02 — Enthusiastic Agreement After Explanation

**Scenario:** After the agent explains the rationale for an intervention, the user responds with strong agreement and enthusiasm.

**Behavioral core:** Strong agreement + enthusiastic acceptance.

**Boundary:** The positive reaction is directed toward the intervention rather than merely acknowledging the explanation.

## VP-01-S03 — Immediate Enthusiastic Willingness

**Scenario:** The user immediately expresses strong willingness to cooperate with the recommended action.

**Behavioral core:** Strong willingness + enthusiasm.

**Boundary:** The response contains more than simple willingness; positive engagement is clearly elevated.

## VP-01-S04 — Strong Positive Reaction to Helpful Intervention

**Scenario:** The user recognizes that the intervention is helpful and responds with strong positive acceptance.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + strong positive appraisal.

**Boundary:** The reaction is substantially more positive than routine cooperation.

## VP-01-S05 — Enthusiastic Acceptance of Follow-Up Action

**Scenario:** The user enthusiastically accepts a follow-up action proposed by the agent.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + eagerness toward continued action.

**Boundary:** The user actively welcomes the proposed next step.

---

# 3. VP-02 — Strong Appreciation

## VP-02-S01 — Strong Appreciation of Help

**Scenario:** The user strongly expresses appreciation for the intervention because they perceive it as useful.

**Behavioral core:** Strong appreciation + positive reception.

**Boundary:** Appreciation is directed toward the intervention and is behaviorally meaningful.

## VP-02-S02 — Recognition of Significant Helpfulness

**Scenario:** The user explicitly recognizes that the agent's intervention has been particularly helpful.

**Behavioral core:** Strong positive appraisal of intervention value.

**Boundary:** The response reflects substantial appreciation rather than routine politeness.

## VP-02-S03 — Appreciation After Successful Guidance

**Scenario:** Following useful guidance from the agent, the user expresses strong appreciation and positive acceptance of the intervention.

**Behavioral core:** Appreciation + positive reception.

**Boundary:** The appreciation is tied to the intervention's usefulness.

## VP-02-S04 — Strong Gratitude for Persuasive Support

**Scenario:** The user expresses strong gratitude because the intervention helped them understand or accept the recommended action.

**Behavioral core:** Strong gratitude + positive behavioral reception.

**Boundary:** Gratitude must reflect the intervention rather than generic social politeness.

## VP-02-S05 — Appreciation with Continued Willingness

**Scenario:** The user strongly appreciates the intervention and indicates willingness to continue engaging with it.

**Behavioral core:** Strong appreciation + continued cooperation.

**Boundary:** The combination provides stronger evidence than appreciation alone.

---

# 4. VP-03 — Proactive Engagement

## VP-03-S01 — Voluntary Follow-Up Question

**Scenario:** After accepting the intervention, the user voluntarily asks what else they can do to improve or continue.

**Behavioral core:** Positive acceptance + proactive engagement.

**Boundary:** The user extends engagement beyond the immediate requirement.

## VP-03-S02 — Request for Additional Guidance

**Scenario:** The user positively accepts the intervention and actively requests additional guidance.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + voluntary pursuit of further assistance.

**Boundary:** The request reflects active engagement rather than uncertainty alone.

## VP-03-S03 — Voluntary Extension of Interaction

**Scenario:** The user continues the interaction voluntarily after the immediate intervention has been accepted.

**Behavioral core:** Positive reaction + voluntary continuation.

**Boundary:** Continuation is driven by positive engagement with the intervention.

## VP-03-S04 — Self-Initiated Next Step

**Scenario:** The user independently proposes a next step that extends or supports the recommended action.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + proactive action orientation.

**Boundary:** The user contributes voluntarily rather than merely following an instruction.

## VP-03-S05 — Positive Engagement with Broader Goal

**Scenario:** The user accepts the intervention and voluntarily connects it to a broader effort to improve their behavior or routine.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + proactive engagement with the broader objective.

**Boundary:** The user's engagement goes beyond the immediate intervention.

---

# 5. VP-04 — Strong Voluntary Cooperation

## VP-04-S01 — Voluntary Commitment to Act

**Scenario:** The user willingly commits to carrying out the recommended action without meaningful resistance.

**Behavioral core:** Strong cooperation + voluntary commitment.

**Boundary:** Cooperation is clearly stronger than ordinary passive acceptance.

## VP-04-S02 — Voluntary Agreement to Recommended Routine

**Scenario:** The user willingly agrees to incorporate the recommendation into their routine.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + voluntary cooperation.

**Boundary:** The user demonstrates genuine willingness rather than reluctant compliance.

## VP-04-S03 — Willing Cooperation Without Pressure

**Scenario:** The user cooperates with the intervention even though the agent has not applied pressure or repeated persuasion.

**Behavioral core:** Voluntary cooperation + strong acceptance.

**Boundary:** The response indicates intrinsic willingness toward the intervention.

## VP-04-S04 — Voluntary Follow-Through Intention

**Scenario:** The user clearly states a voluntary intention to follow through with the recommended action.

**Behavioral core:** Strong willingness + cooperation + intended follow-through.

**Boundary:** The commitment is voluntary and positive rather than merely conditional or reluctant.

## VP-04-S05 — Strong Cooperation with Continued Intervention

**Scenario:** The user willingly cooperates with the current intervention and welcomes continued assistance.

**Behavioral core:** Strong cooperation + acceptance of continued intervention.

**Boundary:** The user not only accepts the current recommendation but also remains positively receptive to further intervention.

# 6. Cross-Scenario Coverage

| Subtype | Primary semantic focus |
|---|---|
| `VP-01` | Enthusiasm and strong acceptance |
| `VP-02` | Strong appreciation and positive appraisal |
| `VP-03` | Proactive, self-initiated engagement |
| `VP-04` | Strong voluntary cooperation |

The inventory varies the behavioral situation across acceptance, appreciation, usefulness recognition, follow-up engagement, voluntary action, commitment, continued interaction, and proactive participation.

Linguistic realization diversity is introduced separately during Stage 3A.

---

# 7. VP Boundary Coverage

The central distinction is:

```text
P
Ordinary acceptance / cooperation
        ↓
VP
Strong enthusiasm / appreciation /
proactive engagement / strong voluntary cooperation
```

A scenario should not be assigned to `VP` merely because it contains agreement, gratitude, positive sentiment, commitment language, an enthusiastic word, or immediate action.

The behavioral interpretation must support a strongly positive response toward the intervention.

---

# 8. Scenario-Level Semantic Requirements

Each scenario represents one dominant behavioral situation.

The following semantic regions remain distinct:

```text
Enthusiastic Acceptance
        ≠
Strong Appreciation
        ≠
Proactive Engagement
        ≠
Strong Voluntary Cooperation
```

Temporal properties such as immediate or delayed action are scenario dimensions rather than independent Response Category subtypes.

Politeness and wording style are realization characteristics rather than semantic subtype definitions.
