# Stage 1 — Initial Semantic Anchors: P

**Response Category:** `P` — Positive  
**Semantic Subtypes:** 4  
**Semantic Scenarios:** 20  
**Initial Anchors:** 60

---

## 1. Purpose

This file defines initial natural-language anchor realizations for the `P` Response Category.

`P` represents ordinary positive behavioral reaction toward the intervention, characterized by acceptance, cooperation, willingness to act, qualified acceptance, or genuine acceptance that may occur after a delay.

Each semantic scenario has three initial realizations:

1. **Direct** — the behavioral response is clearly expressed.
2. **Indirect** — the behavioral response is conveyed pragmatically without relying on an explicit category marker.
3. **Natural Conversational** — the response resembles ordinary spontaneous conversation.

The anchors are tied to their originating semantic scenarios so that scenario lineage is preserved.

# 4. P-03 — Qualified Acceptance

**Direct:**  
“I can do that, as long as it fits into my schedule.”

**Indirect:**

**Natural Conversational:**

**Direct:**  
“I’ll do it, but I’d prefer to adjust the timing.”

**Indirect:**  
“The approach works better for me with a small change.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Sure, I’ll do it. I’d just rather do it later.”

## P-03-S03 — Conditional Willingness Without Resistance

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Yeah, I can try it as long as it’s manageable.”

# 7. Anchor Construction Principles

The anchors collectively represent:

- ordinary acceptance;
- cooperative agreement;
- willingness to act;
- straightforward commitment;
- practical cooperation;
- qualified acceptance;
- acceptance with minor modifications;
- conditional willingness without meaningful resistance;
- genuine acceptance after consideration;
- genuine acceptance after previous uncertainty or hesitation.

The three realizations for each scenario preserve the same underlying behavioral meaning while varying how explicitly that meaning is expressed.

The `P` anchors are distinguished from `VP` by the absence of strong enthusiasm, unusually high appreciation, proactive engagement, or strong voluntary cooperation.

The `P` anchors are distinguished from `I` by sufficient behavioral evidence of acceptance or cooperation.

Qualified acceptance remains `P` when the qualification does not constitute meaningful resistance.

Delayed acceptance remains `P` when the eventual response contains genuine behavioral willingness. Delay itself does not make a response `I`.

Expressions such as “okay,” “sure,” “I’ll,” or “that sounds reasonable” are not intended to function as deterministic lexical indicators of `P`.
