# Stage 1 — Initial Semantic Anchors: I

**Response Category:** `I` — Indifferent  
**Semantic Subtypes:** 4  
**Semantic Scenarios:** 20  
**Initial Anchors:** 60

---

## 1. Purpose

This file defines initial natural-language anchor realizations for the `I` Response Category.

`I` represents a response in which there is insufficient behavioral evidence for clear acceptance or clear resistance toward the intervention. It includes neutral acknowledgement, uncertainty, non-commitment, and situations where the available response does not establish a sufficiently strong behavioral position.

Each semantic scenario has three initial realizations:

1. **Direct** — the behavioral position is clearly expressed.
2. **Indirect** — the behavioral position is conveyed pragmatically without relying on an explicit category marker.
3. **Natural Conversational** — the response resembles ordinary spontaneous conversation.

The anchors are tied to their originating semantic scenarios so that scenario lineage is preserved.

**Indirect:**

**Natural Conversational:**

# 3. I-02 — Uncertain Position

## I-02-S01 — Uncertainty About Whether to Act

**Direct:**  
“I’m not sure whether I’ll do it.”

**Indirect:**  
“I haven’t decided what I want to do yet.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’m not really sure yet.”

## I-02-S02 — Uncertain Evaluation of Recommendation

**Direct:**  
“I’m not sure if that recommendation is right for me.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’m still trying to decide whether it makes sense for me.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Hmm, I’m not sure about that yet.”

**Direct:**  
“I need some time to think about it.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’d rather make the decision after I’ve considered it.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Let me think about it for a bit.”

“I can see both sides, and I haven’t decided.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’m still weighing the options.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I don’t know yet. I’m still thinking.”

“I’ll see how it goes.”

# 7. Anchor Construction Principles

The anchors collectively represent:

- neutral acknowledgement;
- receipt of information;
- acknowledgement without acceptance;
- uncertainty about whether to act;
- uncertainty about the recommendation;
- delayed decision;
- non-commitment;
- deferred commitment;
- insufficient behavioral evidence.

The three realizations for each scenario preserve the same underlying behavioral meaning while varying how explicitly that meaning is expressed.

The `I` anchors are distinguished from `P` by the absence of sufficiently clear behavioral commitment, acceptance, or cooperation.

The `I` anchors are distinguished from `N` by the absence of sufficiently clear behavioral resistance, refusal, or disagreement.

A response may contain uncertainty, delay, or non-commitment without being negative. Conversely, a delayed response that contains genuine behavioral acceptance should belong to `P`, while a delayed response expressing resistance should belong to `N`.

Minimal acknowledgements such as “Okay,” “Alright,” or “Hmm” are included only as examples of insufficient behavioral evidence. Their category cannot be determined from the lexical form alone and must remain context-sensitive during annotation.
