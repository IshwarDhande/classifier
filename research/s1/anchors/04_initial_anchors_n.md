# Stage 1 — Initial Semantic Anchors: N

**Response Category:** `N` — Negative  
**Semantic Subtypes:** 4  
**Semantic Scenarios:** 20  
**Initial Anchors:** 60

---

## 1. Purpose

This file defines initial natural-language anchor realizations for the `N` Response Category.

`N` represents a negative behavioral reaction toward the intervention characterized by reluctance, mild refusal, disagreement, or conditional resistance that remains potentially recoverable.

Each semantic scenario has three initial realizations:

1. **Direct** — the behavioral response is clearly expressed.
2. **Indirect** — the behavioral response is conveyed pragmatically without relying on an explicit category marker.
3. **Natural Conversational** — the response resembles ordinary spontaneous conversation.

The anchors are tied to their originating semantic scenarios so that scenario lineage is preserved.

# 2. N-01 — Reluctance

## N-01-S01 — Hesitant Willingness

**Direct:**  
“I’m not really comfortable doing that yet.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’d need a little more time before I go ahead with it.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’m a bit hesitant about doing that.”

---

## N-01-S02 — Reluctance to Begin

**Direct:**  
“I don’t really want to start that right now.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’d rather wait before getting started.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’m not really ready to start yet.”

---

## N-01-S03 — Reduced Willingness After Repeated Recommendation

**Direct:**  
“I’m becoming less willing to do this with all the reminders.”

**Indirect:**  
“The more we keep going over it, the less inclined I am to do it.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Honestly, all these reminders are making me less willing to do it.”

---

## N-01-S04 — Reluctant Acceptance of Limited Action

**Direct:**  
“I can do a small part of it, but I’m reluctant to do more.”

**Indirect:**  
“I could probably manage a limited version of that.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I can do a little, but I’m not keen on doing the whole thing.”

---

## N-01-S05 — Hesitation About Continuing the Practice

**Direct:**  
“I’m hesitant about continuing with this.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’m not sure I want to keep doing it.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’m having second thoughts about keeping this up.”

# 3. N-02 — Mild Refusal

## N-02-S01 — Direct Mild Refusal

**Direct:**  
“I’d rather not do that.”

**Indirect:**  
“That’s probably not something I want to take on.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Yeah, I’d rather not.”

---

## N-02-S02 — Refusal of a Specific Recommendation

**Direct:**  
“I’m not going to follow that particular recommendation.”

**Indirect:**  
“I think I’ll take a different approach for that.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I don’t think I’ll do that one.”

---

## N-02-S03 — Declining the Proposed Action for the Present

**Direct:**  
“I’m not going to do it right now.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’d prefer to leave that for another time.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Not right now. I’ll leave it for later.”

---

## N-02-S04 — Mild Refusal with Continued Discussion

**Direct:**  
“I don’t want to do that, but I’m happy to discuss another option.”

**Indirect:**  
“That particular approach doesn’t work for me, though I’m open to alternatives.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’d rather not do that. Is there another way?”

---

## N-02-S05 — Refusal Without Hostility

**Direct:**  
“No, I don’t want to do that.”

**Indirect:**  
“That’s not something I’m willing to take on.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“No, I’d rather skip that.”

---

# 4. N-03 — Disagreement

## N-03-S01 — Disagreement with Recommendation

**Direct:**  
“I don’t agree with that recommendation.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’m not convinced that’s the right approach for me.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I don’t think that’s the best way for me to handle it.”

---

## N-03-S02 — Disagreement with Intervention Rationale

**Direct:**  
“I don’t agree with the reasoning behind that suggestion.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’m not convinced the explanation supports that recommendation.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I see your point, but I don’t agree with the reasoning.”

---

## N-03-S03 — Challenge to Recommended Approach

**Direct:**  
“I don’t think that approach is appropriate for me.”

**Indirect:**  
“I’m not convinced that method will work in my situation.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’m not sure that approach is going to work for me.”

---

## N-03-S04 — Preference Against the Recommended Action

**Direct:**  
“I’d prefer not to follow that recommendation.”

**Indirect:**  
“Another option would suit me better.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’d rather go with something else.”

---

## N-03-S05 — Disagreement with Continued Openness

**Direct:**  
“I disagree with the recommendation, but I’m open to discussing alternatives.”

**Indirect:**  
“I don’t think that approach works for me, although I’m willing to consider another one.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I don’t agree with that, but let’s see what else we could do.”

# 5. N-04 — Conditional Resistance

## N-04-S01 — Resistance Under a Current Constraint

**Direct:**  
“I can’t really do that under my current circumstances.”

**Indirect:**  
“That would be difficult for me with things as they are right now.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I don’t think I can manage that right now.”

---

## N-04-S02 — Willingness Only Under a Specific Condition

**Direct:**  
“I could do it if the timing were changed.”

**Indirect:**  
“That might work for me under different conditions.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I could probably do it if we moved it to another time.”

---

## N-04-S03 — Resistance to Current Form of Intervention

**Direct:**  
“I don’t want to do it in that way.”

**Indirect:**  
“The current approach doesn’t work for me, but another approach might.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I’m not comfortable with that version. Maybe we could try something else.”

---

## N-04-S04 — Conditional Cooperation After Adjustment

**Direct:**  
“I’ll consider doing it if the recommendation is adjusted.”

**Indirect:**  
“A small change would make me more willing to try it.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“I could give it a shot if we changed that part.”

---

## N-04-S05 — Temporary Resistance

**Direct:**  
“I’m not willing to do it at the moment, but that could change later.”

**Indirect:**  
“This isn’t a good time for me, although I may reconsider.”

**Natural Conversational:**  
“Not for now. I might be open to it later.”

# 7. Anchor Construction Principles

The anchors collectively represent:

- hesitation;
- reduced willingness;
- reluctance to begin or continue;
- mild refusal;
- refusal of a specific recommendation;
- disagreement;
- preference for an alternative;
- resistance under current conditions;
- conditional willingness;
- temporary resistance;
- resistance that remains potentially recoverable.

The three realizations for each scenario preserve the same underlying behavioral meaning while varying how explicitly that meaning is expressed.

The `N` anchors are distinguished from `I` by the presence of meaningful behavioral resistance, refusal, or disagreement.

The `N` anchors are distinguished from `VN` by the absence of strong rejection, hostility, high reactance, or active disengagement.

A negative emotional expression alone does not establish `N`. Likewise, words such as “no,” “not,” “rather not,” or “can’t” should not independently determine the category.

Where the response remains open to alternatives, future cooperation, adjustment, or continued discussion, the underlying behavioral state remains compatible with `N` rather than automatically escalating to `VN`.
