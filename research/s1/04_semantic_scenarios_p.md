# Stage 1 — Semantic Scenario Inventory: P

**Response Category:** `P` — Positive  
**Semantic Subtypes:** 4  
**Scenario Count:** 20

---

## 1. Purpose

This inventory defines distinct behavioral situations in which a user's response belongs to the `P` Response Category.

`P` represents acceptance, cooperation, willingness, or genuine commitment without the strong enthusiasm, appreciation, proactive engagement, or strong voluntary positive intensity required for `VP`.

The scenarios below describe semantic situations, not linguistic realizations.

---

# 2. P-01 — Ordinary Acceptance

## P-01-S01 — Straightforward Acceptance

**Scenario:** The user accepts the recommended action in a straightforward manner without expressing strong enthusiasm.

**Behavioral core:** Clear acceptance + low resistance.

**Boundary:** Positive because acceptance is evident; not Very Positive because strong enthusiasm or appreciation is absent.

## P-01-S02 — Cooperative Agreement

**Scenario:** The user agrees to the intervention and indicates ordinary willingness to cooperate.

**Behavioral core:** Agreement + cooperation.

**Boundary:** Cooperation is genuine but not unusually strong or proactive.

## P-01-S03 — Acceptance of a Routine Recommendation

**Scenario:** The user accepts a routine recommendation as a reasonable action to take.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + ordinary willingness.

**Boundary:** The user accepts the intervention without strong positive appraisal.

## P-01-S04 — Positive Response Without Strong Enthusiasm

**Scenario:** The user responds positively to the intervention and is willing to proceed, but shows no strong enthusiasm.

**Behavioral core:** Positive reception + willingness.

**Boundary:** The response remains below the intensity required for VP.

## P-01-S05 — Simple Acceptance After Brief Consideration

**Scenario:** After briefly considering the intervention, the user accepts it without meaningful resistance.

**Behavioral core:** Considered acceptance + low resistance.

**Boundary:** The response demonstrates acceptance rather than uncertainty or reluctance.

---

# 3. P-02 — Cooperative Commitment

## P-02-S01 — Clear Commitment to Follow Through

**Scenario:** The user accepts the recommendation and clearly commits to carrying it out.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + behavioral commitment.

**Boundary:** Commitment provides stronger evidence of cooperation than simple acknowledgement.

## P-02-S02 — Commitment to a Recommended Routine

**Scenario:** The user agrees to incorporate the recommended behavior into their routine.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + intended follow-through.

**Boundary:** The commitment is genuine and cooperative rather than reluctant.

## P-02-S03 — Commitment to Complete a Specific Action

**Scenario:** The user explicitly commits to completing the action recommended by the agent.

**Behavioral core:** Clear behavioral intention + cooperation.

**Boundary:** The commitment is positive but not necessarily enthusiastic.

## P-02-S04 — Agreement to Continue the Recommended Practice

**Scenario:** The user agrees to continue following a recommended practice.

**Behavioral core:** Sustained willingness + cooperation.

**Boundary:** Continued commitment distinguishes the scenario from a one-time acknowledgement.

## P-02-S05 — Commitment After Clarification

**Scenario:** After receiving clarification about the intervention, the user understands the recommendation and commits to following it.

**Behavioral core:** Understanding + acceptance + commitment.

**Boundary:** Clarification resolves uncertainty and results in genuine cooperation.

---

# 4. P-03 — Qualified Acceptance

## P-03-S01 — Acceptance with a Practical Condition

**Scenario:** The user accepts the intervention but identifies a practical condition under which they can carry it out.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + condition + continued cooperation.

**Boundary:** The condition does not become dominant resistance.

## P-03-S02 — Acceptance with a Minor Reservation

**Scenario:** The user accepts the recommendation while expressing a minor reservation.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + limited reservation.

**Boundary:** Acceptance remains behaviorally dominant.

## P-03-S03 — Conditional Willingness

**Scenario:** The user indicates willingness to cooperate if a specified condition is met.

**Behavioral core:** Conditional acceptance + potential cooperation.

**Boundary:** The condition still leaves genuine willingness to cooperate; it is not a refusal.

## P-03-S04 — Acceptance with Limited Constraint

**Scenario:** The user accepts the intervention but notes a limitation that may affect how they carry it out.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + constraint + continued willingness.

**Boundary:** The limitation does not overturn the positive behavioral response.

## P-03-S05 — Acceptance with Mild Concern

**Scenario:** The user accepts the recommendation while mentioning a concern that does not prevent cooperation.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + mild concern.

**Boundary:** The concern is subordinate to the user's willingness to cooperate.

---

# 5. P-04 — Delayed Genuine Acceptance

## P-04-S01 — Acceptance with Later Action

**Scenario:** The user accepts the intervention but indicates that they will act at a later time.

**Behavioral core:** Genuine acceptance + delayed action.

**Boundary:** The delay does not remove evidence of willingness.

## P-04-S02 — Commitment to Act Later

**Scenario:** The user commits to carrying out the recommended action but schedules it for a later time.

**Behavioral core:** Commitment + future action.

**Boundary:** The response remains positive because commitment is clear.

## P-04-S03 — Acceptance After Current Constraint

**Scenario:** The user accepts the recommendation but cannot act immediately because of a current practical constraint.

**Behavioral core:** Acceptance + temporary constraint + future cooperation.

**Boundary:** The constraint explains the delay without creating behavioral resistance.

## P-04-S04 — Future Follow-Through Intention

**Scenario:** The user expresses genuine intention to follow through with the intervention at a later point.

**Behavioral core:** Future cooperation + genuine willingness.

**Boundary:** Future orientation does not become Indifferent when willingness is explicit.

## P-04-S05 — Deferred but Genuine Cooperation

**Scenario:** The user postpones the recommended action while clearly maintaining willingness to cooperate.

**Behavioral core:** Delayed action + sustained cooperation.

**Boundary:** Postponement is distinct from avoidance, refusal, or uncertainty.

---

# 6. Cross-Scenario Coverage

| Subtype | Primary semantic focus |
|---|---|
| `P-01` | Ordinary acceptance and cooperation |
| `P-02` | Acceptance with clear behavioral commitment |
| `P-03` | Acceptance with non-dominant conditions or reservations |
| `P-04` | Genuine acceptance with delayed action |

The inventory covers:

- straightforward acceptance;
- ordinary cooperation;
- genuine commitment;
- continued cooperation;
- conditional acceptance;
- minor reservations;
- practical constraints;
- delayed but genuine action.

Linguistic realization diversity is introduced separately during Stage 3A.

---

# 7. P Boundary Coverage

The central distinction is:

```text
I
No sufficient behavioral evidence of acceptance
        ↓
P
Acceptance / cooperation is behaviorally evident
        ↓
VP
Strong enthusiasm, appreciation, proactive engagement,
or strong voluntary positive engagement
```

The presence of commitment does not automatically make a response `VP`. Commitment can remain `P` when enthusiasm or unusually strong positive engagement is absent.

Similarly, delay does not automatically make a response `I` when genuine acceptance remains clear.

---

# 8. Scenario-Level Semantic Requirements

Each scenario represents one dominant behavioral situation.

The following semantic regions remain distinct:

```text
Ordinary Acceptance
        ≠
Cooperative Commitment
        ≠
Qualified Acceptance
        ≠
Delayed Genuine Acceptance
```

Conditions, reservations, and delayed action are included only when acceptance remains behaviorally dominant.

Politeness, wording style, and specific lexical forms are realization characteristics rather than semantic subtype definitions.
