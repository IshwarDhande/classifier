# Stage 1 — Semantic Scenario Inventory: N

**Response Category:** `N` — Negative  
**Semantic Subtypes:** 4  
**Scenario Count:** 20

---

## 1. Purpose

This inventory defines distinct behavioral situations in which a user's response belongs to the `N` Response Category.

`N` represents reluctance, disagreement, mild refusal, or meaningful behavioral resistance that remains potentially recoverable and does not reach the strong rejection, hostility, high reactance, or active disengagement associated with `VN`.

The scenarios below describe semantic situations, not linguistic realizations.

---

# 2. N-01 — Reluctance

## N-01-S01 — Hesitant Willingness

**Scenario:** The user shows hesitation about following the intervention while remaining potentially willing to cooperate.

**Behavioral core:** Reduced willingness + recoverable resistance.

**Boundary:** The hesitation is behaviorally meaningful but does not constitute strong rejection.

## N-01-S02 — Reluctance to Begin

**Scenario:** The user is reluctant to start the recommended action despite remaining engaged with the intervention.

**Behavioral core:** Reduced willingness to initiate action.

**Boundary:** The user has not strongly rejected the intervention.

## N-01-S03 — Reduced Willingness After Repeated Recommendation

**Scenario:** Repeated intervention causes the user to become less willing to cooperate, but they remain engaged in the conversation.

**Behavioral core:** Increased reluctance + continued interaction.

**Boundary:** Resistance is present without strong reactance or disengagement.

## N-01-S04 — Reluctant Acceptance of Limited Action

**Scenario:** The user is willing to consider or perform a limited version of the recommended action but expresses reluctance toward broader cooperation.

**Behavioral core:** Partial willingness + resistance.

**Boundary:** Some cooperation remains possible, preventing stronger rejection.

## N-01-S05 — Hesitation About Continuing the Recommended Practice

**Scenario:** The user expresses reluctance about continuing a recommended practice while remaining open to discussion.

**Behavioral core:** Reduced willingness + continued engagement.

**Boundary:** The user has not moved to explicit strong rejection.

---

# 3. N-02 — Mild Refusal

## N-02-S01 — Direct Mild Refusal

**Scenario:** The user declines the recommended action without hostility or strong opposition.

**Behavioral core:** Clear refusal + recoverable interaction.

**Boundary:** The refusal is negative but lacks VN-level intensity.

## N-02-S02 — Refusal of a Specific Recommendation

**Scenario:** The user refuses a particular recommendation while remaining willing to continue interacting.

**Behavioral core:** Specific refusal + continued engagement.

**Boundary:** Rejection is limited to the intervention or action rather than the entire interaction.

## N-02-S03 — Declining the Proposed Action for the Present

**Scenario:** The user declines to perform the recommended action at the present time without strongly rejecting future interaction.

**Behavioral core:** Present refusal + limited resistance.

**Boundary:** The response does not establish strong future rejection.

## N-02-S04 — Mild Refusal with Continued Discussion

**Scenario:** The user refuses the recommendation but continues discussing the issue with the agent.

**Behavioral core:** Refusal + continued interaction.

**Boundary:** Continued engagement indicates that the interaction remains potentially recoverable.

## N-02-S05 — Refusal Without Hostility

**Scenario:** The user clearly declines the intervention while maintaining a non-hostile interaction.

**Behavioral core:** Clear refusal + low hostility.

**Boundary:** Absence of hostility helps distinguish the response from VN.

---

# 4. N-03 — Disagreement

## N-03-S01 — Disagreement with Recommendation

**Scenario:** The user disagrees with the agent's recommendation but remains engaged in the conversation.

**Behavioral core:** Disagreement + continued interaction.

**Boundary:** Disagreement is behaviorally negative but does not imply strong rejection.

## N-03-S02 — Disagreement with Intervention Rationale

**Scenario:** The user disagrees with the reasoning or rationale supporting the intervention.

**Behavioral core:** Opposition to rationale + continued engagement.

**Boundary:** The disagreement remains focused on the intervention rather than terminating the interaction.

## N-03-S03 — Challenge to Recommended Approach

**Scenario:** The user challenges the proposed approach and indicates that they do not agree with it.

**Behavioral core:** Behavioral disagreement + resistance.

**Boundary:** The user remains engaged rather than actively disengaging.

## N-03-S04 — Preference Against the Recommended Action

**Scenario:** The user indicates that they would prefer not to follow the recommended action.

**Behavioral core:** Negative preference + reduced cooperation.

**Boundary:** The response expresses resistance but does not necessarily reject the entire interaction.

## N-03-S05 — Disagreement with Continued Openness

**Scenario:** The user disagrees with the recommendation while remaining open to discussing alternatives.

**Behavioral core:** Disagreement + recoverable interaction.

**Boundary:** Continued openness prevents the response from reaching strong opposition or disengagement.

---

# 5. N-04 — Conditional Resistance

## N-04-S01 — Resistance Under a Current Constraint

**Scenario:** The user resists the intervention because a current circumstance prevents them from cooperating.

**Behavioral core:** Resistance + contextual condition.

**Boundary:** The user may cooperate if the condition changes.

## N-04-S02 — Willingness Only Under a Specific Condition

**Scenario:** The user indicates that they will consider cooperation only if a stated condition is satisfied.

**Behavioral core:** Conditional willingness + resistance.

**Boundary:** The response remains potentially recoverable because cooperation is not completely rejected.

## N-04-S03 — Resistance to Current Form of Intervention

**Scenario:** The user resists the current form of the intervention while leaving open the possibility of accepting an alternative.

**Behavioral core:** Resistance to current approach + conditional openness.

**Boundary:** The user rejects the current approach rather than all possible assistance.

## N-04-S04 — Conditional Cooperation After Adjustment

**Scenario:** The user indicates that cooperation may be possible if the recommendation is modified.

**Behavioral core:** Resistance + potential cooperation after adjustment.

**Boundary:** The resistance is directed toward the current intervention rather than interaction as a whole.

## N-04-S05 — Temporary Resistance

**Scenario:** The user resists the intervention for the present but does not express strong opposition to future cooperation.

**Behavioral core:** Temporary resistance + future recoverability.

**Boundary:** The response remains below strong rejection or active disengagement.

# 6. Cross-Scenario Coverage

| Subtype | Primary semantic focus |

| `N-02` | Mild refusal |
| `N-03` | Disagreement with the intervention |
| `N-04` | Conditional or recoverable resistance |

The inventory covers:

- hesitation;
- reduced willingness;
- mild refusal;
- disagreement;
- preference against an intervention;
- conditional resistance;
- temporary resistance;
- resistance to a specific intervention form;
- resistance while remaining engaged;
- resistance that can potentially be resolved.

Linguistic realization diversity is introduced separately during Stage 3A.

---

# 7. N Boundary Coverage

The central distinctions are:

```text
I
No meaningful behavioral resistance
        ↓
N
Behaviorally meaningful resistance
        ↓
VN
Strong rejection, opposition, reactance,
or active disengagement
```

The following distinctions are especially important:

```text
Uncertainty ≠ automatically N
Disagreement ≠ automatically VN
Mild refusal ≠ automatically VN
Temporary resistance ≠ automatically VN
Negative emotion ≠ automatically N
```

The behavioral resistance must be directed toward the intervention and must be strong enough to distinguish the response from Indifferent.

---

# 8. Scenario-Level Semantic Requirements

Each scenario represents one dominant behavioral situation.

The following semantic regions remain distinct:

```text
Reluctance
        ≠
Mild Refusal
        ≠
Disagreement
        ≠
Conditional Resistance
```

The distinction is based on the user's behavioral relationship with the intervention rather than the presence of negative words or emotion.

Resistance remains within `N` when the interaction is potentially recoverable and the response does not demonstrate strong rejection, hostility, high reactance, or active disengagement.
